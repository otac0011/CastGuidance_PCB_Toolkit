RF and microwave

Matching networks, attenuators and planar passives.

LC Matching Ladder (value-solver)

The most capable tool in the toolkit, and the one with the most inputs. It solves component values for a strictly-alternating LC ladder that matches a complex source to a complex load. The engine solves values and reports feasibility — it never changes your topology. You decide the shape; it tells you what parts go in it and whether they will work.

Choosing the topology

Element scheme. Series-L / Shunt-C is a low-pass ladder (series legs are inductors, shunt legs are capacitors). Series-C / Shunt-L is the high-pass dual.

First element. Series-first or shunt-first, on the source side. This sets which end the shunt element sits on, and that flips the direction of the impedance transformation: series-first puts the shunt toward the load (for a load above the reference resistance), shunt-first puts it on the source side (for a load below it). The rule is that the shunt element belongs across the higher-resistance side. The tool raises an advisory if your chosen orientation is the opposite of what the terminations call for.

Number of legs. 1 to 12. One leg is an L-network. More legs buy bandwidth and lower node Q at the cost of parts and loss.

Inputs

Complex source Zs and load Zl as R + jX, a real Z₀ reference, the target centre frequency f₀, a sweep band for the plot, the matching objective (reflectionless to Z₀, or conjugate to the source), and the pass target for |S11|.

The three calculation modes

  1. Raw ideal — exact mathematical values, no parasitics.
  2. Ideal snapped — those values rounded to the purchasable RF grid.
  3. Purchasable re-solved — the values re-solved so that the real network — including body ESL, mounting and solder inductance, pad capacitance and ESR — meets the target at f₀.

Mode 3 is the one that matters. Snapping an ideal answer to stock values and hoping is how matching networks come back off the bench 10 % low.

Component bounds and parasitics

Values are bounded to the typical RF window even for the theoretical answer — Coilcraft 0402DC inductors 0.8–120 nH, Murata GJM1555 C0G capacitors 1–100 pF. Sub-picofarad capacitors are excluded because you cannot buy or place them reliably.

Capacitor and inductor packages are chosen independently — an 0201 capacitor with an 0402 inductor is a normal combination — and each type's bounds and parasitics are tracked from its own package.

Parasitics are value-dependent: a larger inductor has higher DCR and a lower self-resonant frequency, so each candidate design is evaluated with its own SRF, series loss and Q, on top of the fixed pad, package and solder parasitics.

The component-value spread slider

For 3 or more legs there is a family of solutions rather than a single answer. The slider sweeps that family from the smallest L/C that still matches to the largest. Only positions that actually meet the pass |S11| target in the selected mode are shown — infeasible positions are dropped — so every stop on the slider is a valid design. A 1- or 2-element match is fully determined, so the slider is disabled there.

Outputs

Per-leg component values with node Q and the value-dependent SRF / Rs / Q at f₀; |S11|, return loss and VSWR at f₀; network Q and minimum Q; the numerically-measured matched bandwidth; an S11-versus-frequency plot with the target line; and for 3+ legs the smallest-to-largest component-value family.

Export as a 2-port Touchstone .s2p (or .s1p), HTML, or TXT.

Insight. Node Q is the number that predicts how touchy the network will be. A high-Q node means a narrow match that moves when a component is 5 % off or a neighbouring copper pour changes the pad capacitance. If the solver offers you a low-Q solution at the same |S11|, take it.

S-parameter quality. The exported .s2p is screened before you see it: passivity (the largest singular value of S may not exceed 1), reciprocity (S12 = S21), sampling (phase step between points) and causality (sign of the group delay). The verdict is shown as S-param quality under Performance, written into every Touchstone file as a comment line, and a failing check appears as a diagnostic. The .s2p holds the network alone, Z₀ at both ports; the .s1p is the input reflection with your load attached.

Not yet available. The Smith chart view is planned for a future update.

Real-part build

Under the outputs, the Real-part build card answers "what happens when I buy these parts". It starts from the ideal values of the design on screen and leaves the three calculation modes unchanged.

  1. With part parasitics (on by default): each part carries its package's parasitics and the values are re-tuned with them. A capacitor has mounted ESL that falls with value, ESR rising as √f and C0G loss. An inductor is the package's wirewound RF part: DC resistance, k·√f loss and self-capacitance. Parasitics make a part look larger, so the re-tuned values come out smaller.
  2. Preferred values: each value is snapped to the IEC 60063 series you pick (E24 by default). Nearest is the closest value. Chosen is the best combination of each leg's two neighbouring values.
  3. Shunt ground via: the inductance from each shunt part to the plane (0 = ideal ground). A 0.3 nH via is +j4.6 Ω at 2.45 GHz.

Each row shows the ideal, tuned, nearest and chosen value, and the chosen part's SRF, tolerance and Q at f₀. A green |S11| plot shows the chosen build. A part used past its self-resonance is flagged.

Real-part models and tolerance yield

The part models are FishLadder's package-level generic models. They were checked against Johanson's measured series-through library and the RF wirewound datasheets. They are estimates, not a vendor part, so sign-off still needs the vendor's S-parameters.

Tolerance yield. A Monte Carlo of 2000 builds, each part uniform within its tolerance. It uses a fixed seed (20260806), so the same design always gives the same yield. It is judged against the pass target at f₀, or over f₀ ± 2.5 % or ± 5 %. Small parts use the wider absolute bin: ±0.1 pF is ±10 % of a 1 pF part. Tolerance moves only the value; the parasitics stay those of the nominal part.

The card also shows the exact worst ± corner, and lists the parts to tighten first: the ones whose tolerance alone costs the most return loss.

Quarter-Wave Match (multi-section)

A cascaded λ/4 transformer for matching two real impedances, with the microstrip widths solved for you.

Methodology dropdown. Binomial (maximally flat, monotonic VSWR — clean in-band response) or Chebyshev (equal-ripple — wider bandwidth at the cost of in-band ripple).

Inputs. Source Z and load Z (both must be real), design frequency, substrate height h and copper thickness t for the width solver, the number of sections (1–9), and the VSWR criterion that defines the bandwidth edge (1.5:1, 2:1 or 3:1).

Outputs. Fractional bandwidth, the frequency range that meets your VSWR criterion, total transformer length, a per-section table of impedance / width / length, and a VSWR-versus-frequency plot.

Insight. Each extra section roughly doubles the usable bandwidth but adds a quarter wavelength of board. At 2.4 GHz on FR-4 a section is about 17 mm, so a 4-section transformer is nearly 70 mm of trace — often the reason a lumped LC match wins on a small board.

Real impedances only. If your load has reactance, either cancel it first with a series element or use the LC Matching Ladder, which handles complex terminations directly.

Pi-Pad Attenuator

A resistive Pi-network attenuator (shunt → series → shunt).

Inputs. Attenuation in dB (3, 6, 10, 20, 30 dB are the common values), and Zin and Zout. Equal impedances give a symmetric pad; unequal ones give a matching pad.

Outputs. The three exact resistor values — series, shunt input, shunt output — each snapped to the nearest E24 standard value.

T-Pad Attenuator

The dual topology (series → shunt → series). Same inputs as the Pi-pad; outputs are the two series resistors and the shunt resistor, exact and E24-snapped.

Choosing between them. Electrically they are equivalent. Pi tends to be a better layout fit when you have ground nearby on both ends; T when you do not. At high attenuation, the Pi's series resistor gets very large and the T's shunt resistor gets very small — pick whichever puts your values in a comfortable range.

Insight. Attenuators are the cheapest way to fix a marginal match. 6 dB of pad improves a 3:1 VSWR to about 1.3:1 at the cost of 6 dB of signal, which is often a trade worth making on a test port or a driver output.

Planar Spiral Inductor

Inductance of a planar spiral, via the Modified Wheeler expression (Mohan et al., 1999).

Inputs. Shape (square, hexagonal, octagonal, circular), number of turns N, conductor width W, spacing S between turns, and the outer diameter d_out.

Outputs. Inductance, the back-calculated inner diameter, average diameter d_avg, fill factor ρ, and the shape constants K₁ and K₂ the formula uses.

Insight. Circular spirals have the best inductance-per-area and the highest Q; octagonal is the usual compromise when your layout tool dislikes curves. Fill factor ρ near 0.5 is roughly optimal — pack the turns too tightly and proximity effect eats the Q, spread them too far and you waste area.
