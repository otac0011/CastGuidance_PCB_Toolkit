Reference calculators

Thirteen small tools for the arithmetic you do constantly. No methodology choices, no diagnostics worth arguing about — just fast answers.

Ohm's Law

Enter any two of voltage, current, resistance and power; the other two appear. The four quantities are linked, so there is no solve-mode dropdown. Resistance is also snapped to the nearest E12 and E24 standard value.

Insight. Watch the power figure. If it exceeds 50 % of a typical 0603 resistor's 100 mW rating, move to 1206 or 2010.

Conversion Calculator

Tabbed converter: distance (mil / mm / inch / µm), temperature, power and dBm, rectangular ↔ polar, and degrees ↔ radians. Type into either side of a tab and the other updates.

Handy exact values: 1 mil = 0.0254 mm, 1 inch = 25.4 mm, 0 dBm = 1 mW = 224 mVrms at 50 Ω, 10 dB = 10× power = 3.16× voltage.

SI Prefix Reference

A static table of every SI prefix from quecto (10⁻³⁰) to quetta (10³⁰) with symbol, power of ten and an example. For when you cannot remember whether P is peta or pico.

dBm Reference Chart

A lookup table from −50 dBm to +40 dBm mapping each level to milliwatts and to Vrms, at a system impedance you choose: 50 Ω (RF default), 75 Ω (video/CATV), 600 Ω (legacy audio), or a custom value.

Insight. dBm is an absolute power, so it only maps to a voltage once you fix the impedance. The same 0 dBm is 224 mV at 50 Ω and 774 mV at 600 Ω.

Mechanical Reference

Two static tables with a live filter box.

Drill sizes — every ANSI numbered drill (#80 to #1) and lettered drill (A to Z) with diameter in inch and mm.

Screw threads — every UNC size (#00-90 up to 1/4-20) with major diameter, threads per inch and the recommended tap drill.

Wire Ampacity / Resistance / Thermal

The most substantial of the reference tools — a full wire analyzer.

Wire specification. AWG gauge (4/0 down to 40); conductor material (solid or stranded copper, aluminium 1350, copper-clad aluminium, nichrome, constantan, manganin, tinned or silver-plated copper); insulation class (20 options from PVC 60 °C up to Kapton 260 °C, including the IEC 60317 magnet-wire enamel classes A to R).

Operating conditions. Ambient temperature (inside an enclosure expect 20–40 °C above room), wire length (one-way — do not double it for the return), current (set to 0 to see only the maximum ampacity), source voltage (enables voltage drop as a percentage), and frequency (enables skin depth and the AC resistance bump).

Outputs. Cross-section, diameter, resistance per metre at ambient, total resistance at operating temperature, maximum continuous ampacity colour-coded against the insulation rating, Preece fusing current, the wire's temperature at your current, voltage drop with its percentage of source, power dissipated, skin depth, AC resistance, and mass per metre.

Insight. Wire temperature at your actual current is the number that matters. If it exceeds the insulation's continuous rating the tool raises an explicit OVERHEAT or THERMAL RUNAWAY diagnostic.

Reactance and LC Resonance

Inputs. Frequency, capacitance and/or inductance, and an optional series resistance for the Q calculation.

Outputs. Xc, Xl, the resonant frequency where they are equal, and Q.

Insight. A 100 nF capacitor presents 16 mΩ at 100 MHz and 160 Ω at 10 kHz — the same part, four orders of magnitude apart. Q sets bandwidth: BW ≈ f₀ / Q.

LED Series Resistor

Inputs. Supply voltage, LED forward voltage (1.8–2.0 V red, 2.8–3.4 V blue/white, 3.0–3.5 V power LEDs), desired forward current (10–20 mA for indicators, 350 mA for a 1 W part), and the number of LEDs in series.

Outputs. The exact resistance, the nearest E12 and E24 values, the actual current with the E24 part, the power dissipated, and a recommended resistor package.

Voltage Divider

Four-corner solve: enter any two of Vin, Vout, R1, R2 and the other two follow. Outputs also include total resistance, divider current, and the power in each resistor, with R1 and R2 snapped to E12/E24.

Insight. For an ADC input keep R1 ∥ R2 at or below 10 kΩ so the source impedance does not fight the sampling capacitor.

Series / Parallel R-L-C

A table of up to eight elements. Pick R, L or C, enter the values, and get the series and parallel totals. Values add in series for R and L and in parallel for C; the reciprocals apply the other way round.

Useful for making an odd value out of stock parts, or for paralleling resistors to spread dissipation.

PPM Frequency Tolerance

Bidirectional: enter ppm or Δf and get the other, along with f_min and f_max.

Insight. USB and CAN allow ±500 ppm; 1G Ethernet is ±100 ppm; a disciplined OCXO is ±0.001 ppm. A crystal datasheet's headline "tolerance" usually combines initial accuracy at 25 °C, stability over the operating temperature range, and ageing per year — check the breakdown before assuming one number covers all three.

XTAL Load Capacitor

Sizes the two load capacitors for a Pierce oscillator around a parallel-resonant crystal.

Inputs. The crystal's specified load capacitance CL (8, 10, 12, 18, 20 and 32 pF are common), the stray capacitance (pin plus PCB — typically 8–14 pF total, from 5–6 pF per oscillator pin plus 1–3 pF of board), and optionally C1 if you want an asymmetric pair.

Outputs. C1 and C2, plus the symmetric rule-of-thumb value for reference.

Insight. Use C0G/NP0 for load capacitors. X7R drifts several percent over temperature and will detune the oscillator; on a part with a ±10 ppm crystal that is a self-inflicted wound.

Wavelength

λ = c / (f · √ε_eff), with a list of useful fractions.

Inputs. Frequency and ε_eff — use 1 for vacuum or air, roughly 0.65 × Dk for microstrip, and Dk itself for stripline.

Outputs. Full λ, the fractional wavelengths (λ/2, λ/4, λ/8, λ/10, λ/20), and the period in nanoseconds.

Insight. λ/20 is the conventional boundary between lumped and distributed behaviour. Anything longer than λ/20 at your highest frequency of interest needs controlled-impedance treatment.
